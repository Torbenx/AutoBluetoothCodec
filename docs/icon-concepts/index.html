Codec Companion Icons

Six icon concepts that say what the app does: sound going into a codec and coming out as data. The app's own drawing does the talking. The Bluetooth figure mark sits below it as a secondary badge, so a Play Store search for a Bluetooth codec tool can find it.

Before you ship

Open points

Ask the brand manager

Page 7 bars embedding the mark in a licensee logo, and an app icon sits close to that line. The guide sends any use outside its list to brand.manager@bluetooth.com. A short email with two of these icons attached settles it.

Licence comes first

Only Bluetooth SIG members may use the marks under the Trademark License Agreement (p. 4–5). A free Adopter membership covers that. Check what the agreement asks of an app that doesn't ship radio hardware.

Attribution in the listing

Add the p. 6 footnote to the store description: "The Bluetooth® word mark and logos are registered trademarks owned by Bluetooth SIG, Inc. and any use of such marks by [licensee name] is under license."

Have a fallback ready

Every concept works without the badge. If the SIG says no, delete the mark and move the drawing to the centre. Search can still find the app through the name and the description, which may use the word mark.
